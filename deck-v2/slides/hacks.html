Six hacks to pay less

01

Rate-hop

Swap USDC debt at 6.5% for USDT at 4.1%: about $1,200 a year on $50,000.

02

Spread the debt

Borrow USDC, USDT and DAI, so one freeze can’t trap you.

03

Native bridging

Move USDC with Circle CCTP: burn and mint, no wrapped copies.

04

AI co-pilot

Aave’s MCP server reads positions and simulates actions. You still sign.

05

Fix the rate

Morpho Midnight or PWN when you want a known cost and end date.

06

Be the lender

Supply stablecoins on Aave or Morpho, or fund loans on unlloo.com.

0:38 – The money-saving side. 1. Rate-hop: rates differ between stablecoins and chains. On 19 Nov 2026 USDC cost 6.5% and USDT 4.1% on Aave V3 Ethereum: on $50k that is about $1,200 a year. Aave’s debt swap moves the debt in one transaction. One published migration (Lupták) cost about 0.17% of the loan. 2. Spread the debt over several stablecoins (from my old workshop): an issuer freeze or depeg on one doesn’t lock you out. 3. Native bridging: Circle’s CCTP burns USDC on one chain and mints it on another – no third-party bridge token. (The article’s author published an open-source CCTP page, unaudited – test small.) 4. AI co-pilot: Aave runs an official MCP server (mcp.aave.com) that reads markets and positions and simulates actions; it prepares unsigned transactions and never holds keys. Ask “what happens to my HF if BTC drops 25%?”. 5. Fixed rate when you need certainty: Morpho Midnight or PWN. 6. The other side of the trade: supply stablecoins and earn the interest borrowers pay, or lend on Unlloo (unlloo.com) at your own rate – with the risk we discussed: no collateral, so an unpaid loan is a loss. Bonus (old workshop, handle with care): the leverage loop – deposit BTC, borrow stables, buy more BTC, deposit again. It multiplies gains and liquidation risk. For pure leverage, small isolated perps are often cleaner.
