0:43 – The honest version of “loans without collateral”. Smart contracts can liquidate, but they cannot sue you, read a credit file or know who you are. So when the collateral goes away, four other things have to carry the loan. 1. A reputation score. Unlloo’s runs 0–1000: 60% from third-party signals (Human Passport for proof of personhood, Ethos vouches, Talent Protocol, Webacy risk flags, growthepie, Zeru) and 40% from 48 months of wallet history on five chains. Wallets younger than 12 months get nothing from the history part. The contract does not check the score: it is information for lenders, not a gate. 2. Small and short. The blog’s advice for a first crowd loan: 100–500 USDC, 30–90 days, a cap of 8–15% a year, offers open for 7–14 days. Repay on time, and the next request is easier. 3. Someone who knows you. Ask one person to make the first offer before you publish; early offers draw in strangers. And because each loan is isolated, a default falls only on the lenders who funded it – it does not spread to a pool. Isolation concentrates the loss, it does not shrink it. 4. A public record. Every outcome – on time, late, unpaid – is written to the chain. “Without collateral, a smart contract can record a default. It cannot cure one.” Interest keeps accruing, anyone can mark the loan unpaid, and the record follows the wallet. There is no court, no insurance fund. The example on the slide: 1,000 USDC from three lenders, 400 repaid, so each lender loses 60% of their principal. The same shortfall in a 100,000 USDC pool would cost each depositor 0.6%. That is the trade: you choose who you fund, and you carry the whole result. Next: how we got here.
