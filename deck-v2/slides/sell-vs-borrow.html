Part 1 · Why borrow

Same Bitcoin, two choices

You bought 1 BTC at $15,000. Today it’s worth $60,000. You need $20,000 in cash.

Sell ≈ 0.33 BTC

  • A $15,000 gain is realized
  • Capital gains tax: often 15–35%, by country
  • ≈ $2,250–5,250 goes to the state
  • That 0.33 BTC is gone for good

Borrow $20,000

  • Nothing sold: your 1 BTC stays yours
  • LTV 33%, health factor ≈ 2.3
  • Interest ≈ $800–1,300 a year (4–6.5%)
  • If BTC doubles, your collateral doubles too

Illustrative prices and tax rates, not tax advice: check your own country. Interest: USDC and USDT rates on Aave, 19 Nov 2026.

0:07 – Make it concrete with dollars. Scenario: 1 BTC bought at $15k, now $60k. You need $20k for a car, a renovation, a year of life. Option A, sell about a third of a BTC: you realize a $15k gain. What that costs depends on where you pay tax: many countries take somewhere between 15% and 35% of the gain, some take nothing after a holding period, some take more (Slovakia in 2026: 19–35% plus a 16% health levy, per Pavol Lupták’s Opportunist article). At 15–35% that is $2,250–5,250 to the state, and the BTC is gone forever. Ask the room where they pay tax and what their rate is. Option B, borrow $20k against the whole coin: LTV 33%, health factor around 2.3, interest of about $800 to $1,300 a year at today’s dollar rates (USDT about 4.1%, USDC up to 6.5% on Aave V3, 19 Nov 2026). The coin keeps working for you. Honest caveat: borrowing has its own risk – liquidation – and that is what the rest of the hour is about. Rules differ by country; check yours.
