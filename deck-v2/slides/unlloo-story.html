0:45 – The story behind my second project, in two minutes. (Add the hackathon date if you want it on the slide.) 1. It started at the ETHGlobal Prague hackathon with one idea: a loan with no collateral. We did not win. We kept building anyway. 2. The problem we kept coming back to: DeFi lending is a $40B+ market, but every borrower locks $1.50 to $2.00 to borrow $1. That is useful, but it is not credit – “you need money to borrow money, which does not make sense.” Traditional unsecured credit is a $5T+ market. DeFi had almost none of it, because a smart contract cannot take you to court, check a credit file or verify you are a real person. Liquidation was the only enforcement that worked without trust, so everything stayed over-collateralized. 3. Why now: a decade of public on-chain history to score behaviour, proof-of-personhood and zero-knowledge identity tools in production, and reputation networks like Human Passport, Ethos and Talent Protocol. Missing data became a risk-modelling problem instead of a wall. 4. The pivot: Unlloo first ran as one admin-approved lending pool. A human vetted each borrower, a reputation score of 600 was the entry ticket, and lenders shared one blended rate. We moved to crowd loans so that “the people taking the risk make the credit decision”: every lender chooses the borrower, the amount and the rate, and a default stays with that loan’s lenders. What we got wrong (from our May 2026 post): we overestimated how fast lenders would trust reputation data – you cannot learn real default rates without making loans, so we kept the first version small and human-gated. We underestimated the legal work: a legal wrapper takes months per jurisdiction. And we expected pure automation to be ready sooner; guarantor-backed and group lending with real people in the loop look like the realistic path for 2026–27. The May 2026 post also floated conditional KYC (pseudonymous while you repay, identity revealed only on default). That belongs to the earlier design, so don’t present it as a current feature. Quote to close: “If reputation is the input, credit stops being a function of capital and starts being a function of behavior.” Sources (Unlloo blog): – Building under-collateralized loans (May 2026, updated Oct 2026): https://www.unlloo.com/blog/building-under-collateralized-loans – From pools to crowd loans: https://www.unlloo.com/blog/how-unlloo-works-today – The anatomy of on-chain reputation: https://www.unlloo.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-on-chain-reputation Discuss: as a lender, would you rather put money in one shared pool that someone else manages, or pick each borrower yourself? This is exactly the decision we changed our mind on.
