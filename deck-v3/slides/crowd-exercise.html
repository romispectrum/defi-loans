0:50 – Interactive, about 3 minutes. Everything on the left is made up. Read the request aloud, then ask the three questions. Let people answer in pairs, then take two or three answers: how much, which rate, what they would check. Then click to reveal the break-even numbers. Assumptions: 60 days, simple interest, Unlloo keeps 10% of interest, and nothing is recovered on default. Net interest r = APR × 60 ÷ 365 × 0.9; break-even default chance = r ÷ (1 + r). – 8% a year: 1.17% – 10%: 1.46% – 12%: 1.74% – 15%: 2.17% If you honestly expect 2 in 100 loans like this to default, you need a break-even of 2%, which is about 13.8% a year. Below that you lose money on average. Teaching points: (1) the rate you ask for is a price for risk, not a reward; (2) the numbers look small because the term is short, but one default wipes out many loans’ interest; (3) so size every loan as something you can lose entirely, for example 20 to 50 USDC of the 300, and spread across several borrowers.
