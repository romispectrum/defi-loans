0:45 – What a crowd loan is, in one picture. One minute. A normal DeFi loan comes from a pool: you borrow from everyone at once, at one rate set by a formula. A crowd loan comes from named lenders. Several people each lend a part to one borrower, and each chooses their own amount and their own rate. On the slide: lender A offers $500 at 10%, B $300 at 12%, C $200 at 14%. The borrower asked for $1,000 for 90 days, accepts all three and pays 11.4% a year on average. Each lender earns the rate they asked for. Three things to stress: – The borrower sets the frame: the amount, the highest rate they accept, and the dates. – The borrower picks which offers to take. There is no automatic matching. – Nobody lends the whole amount, so nobody carries the whole risk alone. If the loan is not repaid, each lender loses their own part. It is how a group of friends would fund one of them, written into a contract so that strangers can join. The same numbers come back on the “in numbers” slide in a minute. Discuss: would you rather be lender A, B or C here, and why? A bigger part at a lower rate, or a smaller part at a higher one?
