No collateral. What holds the loan together?

Many

Lenders share the risk

Up to 200 lenders fund one loan. Each risks a small part, not the whole amount.

≤ $500

Small and short

Start with $100–500 for 30–90 days, at a rate cap of 8–15%. Repay on time, then borrow more.

1 friend

Someone who knows you

The first offer draws in strangers. Each loan is isolated: a default hits only its lenders.

Forever

A public record

An unpaid loan stays visible on-chain. No courts, no insurance: the lenders lose.

“Without collateral, a smart contract can record a default. It cannot cure one.”

Example: 1,000 USDC from three lenders, 400 repaid. Each lender loses 60% of what they lent.

0:49 – The honest part: what holds a loan together when nothing is locked. One minute, four cards. 1. Many lenders. Up to 200 lenders can fund one loan, each with a small part. Nobody carries the whole risk, and each one decided for themselves. 2. Small and short. First requests work best at 100 to 500 dollars for 30–90 days, with a rate cap of 8–15% a year. Repay on time and the next request is easier. 3. Someone who knows you. Ask one person to make the first offer before you publish: early offers draw in strangers. Each loan is isolated, so a default falls only on the lenders who funded it. 4. A public record. Every outcome – on time, late, unpaid – is written to the chain and follows the wallet. “Without collateral, a smart contract can record a default. It cannot cure one.” There is no court and no insurance fund. The example on the slide: 1,000 USDC from three lenders, 400 repaid, so each lender loses 60% of what they lent. Lenders can also look at the borrower’s wallet history before they fund. Don’t go deeper than that today: the point is the crowd loan itself. Discuss (30 seconds, hands): which of the four would you trust most as a lender, and which not at all? Write down what people say.
