0:14 – Four practical routes for a BTC holder, with a $50k loan as the yardstick. The bars are one year of interest at the low end of each range. 1. Firefish – the peer-to-peer route that feels like a bank. KYC, cash on your bank account, a loan contract. About 50% LTV, 5–15% interest plus 1.5% origination: about $5,250 a year on $50k. Every renewal pays origination again. 2. Morpho – isolated markets, one collateral and one loan asset each. On 19 Nov 2026 WBTC against USDC cost 6.2% on Ethereum and cbBTC against USDC 5.3% on Base: $2,650–3,100 a year. 3. Aave v4 – the new hub-and-spoke version. USDT 4.6% and USDC 5.7% on the Ethereum main spoke, USDT 5.0% on Avalanche: $2,300–2,850 a year. Newest, so the least battle-tested. 4. Aave V3 – the classic, with the deepest liquidity. USDT 4.1% and DAI 4.6% on Ethereum, USDC 4.3% on Arbitrum and 6.5% on Ethereum: $2,050–3,250 a year. The honest summary: the three DeFi routes cost about the same today, 4–6.5%, and the order changes from week to week. In October 2026 USDT on the Aave v4 Avalanche spoke cost about 1.2%; six weeks later it costs 5%. So pick the protocol for its risk and liquidity, then pick the cheapest stablecoin inside it. All rates float. Show the live rates in the demo instead of trusting this slide. Three families behind these routes: – Pool (Aave, Morpho, Compound): a shared pool run by smart contracts. No KYC, repay any time, variable rate. – Peer-to-peer (Firefish, PWN, Morpho Midnight): one lender, fixed rate and end date. Firefish pays cash to your bank, with KYC. PWN accepts almost any collateral, even NFTs. – Custodial CeFi: a company holds your BTC. Celsius and BlockFi went bankrupt in 2022. Not your keys, not your coins.
