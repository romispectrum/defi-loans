Tax: why a loan usually isn’t income

The principle

A loan is money you must give back.

No sale → no realized gain → usually nothing to tax when you borrow.

Keep records: tx hashes, debt tokens, Rotki or Koinly exports.

But watch out

  • Liquidation is a sale, and taxable
  • Closing out can trigger gains (swaps, FX)
  • Rules differ by country: some tax every sale, some exempt long holds
  • EU providers report to tax offices from 2026 (DAC8)

General principle, not tax advice. Rules differ by country and change: ask a tax advisor where you are tax resident. One country as an example: Opportunist on Slovakia.

0:35 – The legal logic, carefully. The principle: when you borrow, you receive money you owe back. It isn’t income and you haven’t sold anything, so in most jurisdictions drawing a loan is not a taxable event. That is the whole “buy, borrow, die” logic. The caveats (be explicit about these): – Liquidation is a disposal of your BTC: a taxable sale. One more reason to keep LTV low. – Repaying or closing the position can have its own consequences – e.g. swaps, or FX differences when you borrowed one stablecoin and repay with another. – For individuals, loan interest usually isn’t deductible. – Tax authorities may not treat a DeFi position like a classic bank loan. Document everything: transaction hashes, the debt token you hold, exports from Rotki, Koinly, CoinTracking or Blockpit, ideally an accountant’s note. – Slovakia, as of 2026 (per Pavol Lupták’s Opportunist article): crypto gains taxed progressively at 19 / 25 / 30 / 35% plus 16% health insurance, and no one-year holding exemption (unlike Czechia). Earlier plans for a 7% rate were reported as rolled back; sources conflict, so verify the current law. – DAC8: since 1 January 2026, EU crypto-asset service providers report user transactions to tax authorities. Say it plainly: this is a general principle, not advice. Talk to a tax advisor about your situation.
