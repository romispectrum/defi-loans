Lender: five checks before you fund

01

Wallet age and rhythm

Months of steady activity. A new wallet gets a small amount.

02

The ask fits the wallet

A request far beyond anything it has handled is a warning.

03

Past loans

Look for liquidations and earlier Unlloo loans: repaid, late or unpaid. The score does not show this.

04

One person, not many wallets

Trace the first funds. Many fresh wallets from one source is a wallet farm.

05

Clean funds

Run a risk screen. Sanctions or hack links are a no.

Does the rate cover the risk?

r ÷ (1 + r)

the break-even chance of default, where r is your net interest

10% a year for one year, minus Unlloo’s 10% fee on interest: you keep 9%. Break-even: if more than about 8 in 100 such loans default, you lose money.

Fund only what you can lose on that one loan.

0:50 – Now the other side of the table: you are a lender. You choose the borrower and the rate, so you do the underwriting. Five checks, from the Unlloo blog’s wallet checklist. 1. Wallet age and rhythm: first outgoing transaction, gaps, counterparties. A recent wallet should get a small amount. 2. The ask fits the wallet: compare the request with the largest past transfers and the balance history. Also check the borrower is not a smart contract that could block repayment. 3. Past loans: debt tokens still outstanding, liquidation events, earlier Unlloo loans marked repaid, late or unpaid. The reputation score does not include this, so check it yourself. 4. One person or many wallets: trace the first incoming funds. A common funder behind many fresh wallets suggests a wallet farm. A Human Passport score above 20 helps but does not rule it out. 5. Clean funds: a 0 to 100 risk screen and a sanctions check. Direct hack or sanctions links are a no; indirect exposure alone is not proof of wrongdoing. Use the score as a filter, not a verdict: check when it was last refreshed and read its factors. It does not capture repayments, liquidations or whether the amount fits the wallet. The price: break-even default share = r ÷ (1 + r), where r is the interest you actually receive over the loan term. With no recovery on default, the loan pays off on average only if fewer than that share of similar loans default. Example from the blog: 10% APR for a year with Unlloo keeping 10% of interest means you keep 9%, so break-even is 0.09 ÷ 1.09, about 8.3%.
