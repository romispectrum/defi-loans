01 · Why borrow

Rich people
don’t sell.
They borrow.

$12.5B

Musk’s loan to buy Twitter in 2022, against $62.5B of Tesla stock: a 20% LTV.

No sale

A loan isn’t a sale or income, so taking one usually isn’t a taxable event.

Short fiat

Owe dollars, keep your BTC. Inflation erodes the debt; the collateral stays yours.

Source: Bitcoin: The Ultimate Collateral (Braiins, 2024), citing Musk’s SEC Schedule 13D/A filing

0:06 – The core idea: buy, borrow, die. Billionaires rarely sell shares. They pledge them at a private bank and borrow against them. They keep the upside, keep voting rights, and don’t realize a capital gain. Example from the Braiins/Firefish book: Musk borrowed $12.5B against $62.5B of Tesla stock to buy Twitter – a 20% LTV. Bitcoin is arguably better collateral than stock or a house: it trades 24/7, it can be verified, moved and sold in minutes, and it can’t burn down. Mental model from my older workshop: a stablecoin loan is a short on fiat. You owe euros or dollars, you hold BTC. If you believe fiat loses value long-term, that is exactly the position you want. Ask the room: who has a mortgage? Congratulations, you are already short fiat.
