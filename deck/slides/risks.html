0:31 – The risk map. Go row by row, one sentence each. – Liquidation is also a taxable sale in most countries, so a liquidation hurts twice. Low LTV, Tokenping alerts (tokenping.xyz) on two channels, and a stablecoin reserve to repay with. – Smart contract and bridge risk: stick to protocols with years of history and many audits; don’t put everything in one protocol or on one chain. – Wrapper risk: WBTC, cbBTC, BTC.b and cirBTC all depend on a custodian; tBTC spreads that trust across a signer group. Know who holds the real BTC, and with a big stack don’t use one wrapper for all of it. – Stablecoin risk: USDC, USDT, EURC and EURCV can be frozen by the issuer; any of them can depeg. DAI is more decentralized. Spreading the debt means one freeze can’t lock you out of repaying. – Rate spikes: in thin markets (Aave v4 spokes, small Morpho markets) the variable rate can jump in hours. – Interest compounds onto the debt: if you never pay anything, HF slowly falls even with BTC flat. – Operational: address poisoning (attackers send dust from look-alike addresses so you copy the wrong one from history), fake frontends, unlimited token approvals (including Permit2). Use bookmarks, an address book, and revoke old approvals at revoke.cash. Self-custody means you are the security team. Use a hardware wallet and test with small amounts first.
