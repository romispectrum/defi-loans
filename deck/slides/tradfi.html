Typical ranges, not quotes. “Lombard” after the bankers of medieval Lombardy, who lent against pledged goods.

0:04 – Before the DeFi part, show that the idea is old. Two loans everyone has heard of. The American mortgage: the house is the collateral. You put 20% down and borrow 80% (that is an 80% LTV), at a rate fixed for 30 years. You keep living in the house; the bank only holds a lien. If the house price falls, nothing happens as long as you keep paying. Stop paying and the bank forecloses: it sells the house to get its money back. The Lombard loan: the private-bank version for people who own stocks, bonds or funds. You pledge the portfolio, the bank lends 50–70% of its value (more against bonds, less against volatile stocks). Nothing is sold, so there is no capital-gains tax, and you keep the dividends and the upside. The catch is the margin call: if the portfolio drops below the agreed level, you top up within days or the bank sells. Named after the Lombard bankers of medieval northern Italy, who lent against pledged goods. The Bitcoin loan is a Lombard loan where the bank is a smart contract: BTC is the collateral, you choose the LTV, and the “margin call” is automatic. We call it the health factor, and it is the number for the rest of the hour. Ask the room: who has a mortgage? Congratulations, you already borrow against an asset.
